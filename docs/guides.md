# Instructor Guides — Integrity Lab

Twelve guides, one per lab scenario. Each assumes the seminar questions and
both `counter` positions are already on the scenario page; this document adds
what those cannot say: how to open the room, what usually happens, and when
to intervene.

**Format rules for using the lab in class**

- 12–20 minutes per scenario. Set the scene aloud (30 seconds), then journal
  time (3 min, in-app or paper), then discussion with the journal closed —
  the argument must survive being spoken to strangers.
- Run the scenario **before** its anchor session if you can (see pairs
  below). A group that argues first, then meets the passage, engages at ten
  times the depth of a group that meets the passage first.
- Both counter-positions get a fair hearing even if the room votes 15–1.
  The one dissenter is often right about what the sutta leaves open.
- No debrief consensus required. The scenario has done its job if the group
  can name exactly where the canon ranks goods and what it leaves open.

## s1 · The group project · Academic → session 3.1

Open on the grade pressure, not the ethics: "whose money is this grade, in a
way?" Expect an early pivot to "why should the free-rider mark up *my*
grade". Treat that as the live question, not venting — it is exactly the
employer/employee duty structure of DN 31, where both parties owe and both
can exit. Intervene if the room splits into "principled" and "pragmatic"
camps; session 3.1's subtraction framing (which behaviours corrupt the work)
cuts across that binary and may rescue the discussion.

## s2 · The citation you didn't read · Academic → session 1.3

Open with hands raised: "how many of your sources have you read past page
two?" The room's tolerance for decorative citation is the fertile ground.
The four great references do double duty here: press the room on *which*
reference this failure is about — the text (the quote versus the actual
sutta) or the person (what the habit makes of you over a career). Expect
"but everyone does it"; do not shame it, make the room articulate what the
fear-driven version of themselves looks like in five years.

## s3 · The AI draft · Academic → session 2.3

Name the tool honestly: some of the room did this last week. Keep the
policy debate out — the question is AN 6.63's, intention as the act. What did
Submitting That Draft intend, and is that different from the act of using a
tool at all? Expect an attempt to outsource the answer to "whatever the
university's rules say"; expose it as a category error, because the passage
locates the act before any rule arrives. If the room splits on whether
prompt-engineering counts as authorship, good — hold it there and let both
sides sharpen.

## s4 · The reference you owe · Work → session 3.2

Open smaller than it looks: "how do you decline to be *quoted*?" before the
dramatic question of false praise. The six-directions frame makes this a
two-party case — the former colleague's direction to you, and yours back.
Push past "be honest, it depends" toward naming which specific duties the
passage lists. The counter-positions hinge on debts created by past favors;
expect and welcome appeals to reciprocity, because the sutta has them too,
and the interesting tension is inside the text, not around it.

## s5 · The exam you could pass · Academic → session 2.5

Do not open on punishment. Open on the ranking: the passage puts
blamelessness above wealth, and the scholarship is the wealth. The realistic
pressure is the family's sacrifice, not the 95. Make the room note that the
curve makes cheating redistributive — others pay the markup — before anyone
converges on "the canon says don't". If the room goes quiet, ask: who has to
know what, in the next ten minutes, for the decision to be fully theirs?
The answer usually comes out in the journals, not the discussion.

## s6 · The mistake you could hide · Work → session 3.1

Open on reveal-time rather than confession: "what does disclosure cost one
week from now, versus one quarter from now?" Use 3.1's subtraction list —
which behaviour, left unsaid, corrupts the working relationship from the
inside? Hear the damage-control argument out fully before countering; it is
stronger than most instructors admit, which makes it a better counterweight.
Then reintroduce the ranking: blamelessness is placed above the goods the
quiet option protects. Ask the room to price what that rank costs *here*,
exactly.

## s7 · Résumé inflation · Career → session 2.5

Open with laughter: "has your CV ever said 'proficient in' anything you
learned on the job the next morning?" This is AN 4.62's four goods in a
career key — the job is wealth, the inflated claim threatens the fourth
good, and the fourth ranks highest. The live wire is scope: where exactly
does "willing to learn" become "already good at"? Large rooms split hard on
this line and do not resolve; that is the intended shape. Name aloud that
the tension is unresolved in the source material itself.

## s8 · The boss's wrong call · Work → session 3.5

Open with: "your manager is not evil; the call is wrong. Same morning."
This steers away from whistleblowing melodrama toward the workable. AN 5.177
governs how business is done (no harm, no misdirection), not who signs the
memo — so the seminar question lands on means rather than on loyalty. Give
the counter-position defending quiet compliance its full length; it is more
defensible than most rooms expect, and hearing it is the point. Close on
which of the five departure/objection options the trade-rules list would
actually rule out.

## s9 · The credit you didn't give · Work → session 2.3

Run it as a two-intention audit: what did the advisor intend, and what did
*you* intend when you didn't push back? AN 6.63's intention-as-act hits
twice, and the room must feel the second hit. Expect a defense of "one
mention in the talk is standard practice"; the counter reads the same norm
as the corruption itself. This scenario pairs well with s3 in a two-part
seminar on intention — one group works authorship, the other attribution —
with a shared plenary comparing the two intention-audits.

## s10 · The badly-paid internship · Work → session 3.2

Write the two direction-pairs on the board — firm to worker, worker to firm —
and have students voice each side; the dissonance does the teaching. There
are two real goods in tension: the internship genuinely grows the worker
(the duty to foster), while underpaying them (the duty to fit wages to
work). AN 8.54 answers both at once, which is why the anchor session is
3.2 rather than something narrower. Expect a stalemate on whether an
underpaid skill-exchange is a fair trade; that stalemate is what the
counter-positions describe, so say so when you get there.

## s11 · The side income · Money → session 1.6

Open on the claim that starts the argument: "a side income is morally
neutral until a buyer loses." This is where the Kālāma material matters —
the passage is a method for handling *positions*, not a rule about trades.
Run the discussion in 1.6's procedure: observe the harm or its absence,
then decide, and make the room name what counts as evidence. Expect a
detour de lord over animal products or alcohol adjacency in the sutta's
trade list; redirect to observed-harm reasoning. This scenario's method
reappears in s8 and s10 if the same group meets them afterward.

## s12 · The gift with strings · Money → session 2.5

Open on cost beyond the purchase price: what does this gift cost besides
the money — objection, friction, the debt created by accepting? The
scenario's pressure comes from that debt; AN 4.62's ranking (blamelessness
above debtlessness) structures the choice but does not settle it, because
refusing a gift can itself create a debt. The two counter-positions
genuinely conflict here, which makes this the best scenario to end
unresolved. Plan for it, and tell the room it is unresolved on purpose.

## s13 · The loan to a friend · Money → session 3.4

Open with the number, not the ethics: "how much could you lose without the
friendship changing?" That question exposes the real variable — not whether
to be generous, but whether the amount on the table can be given without
resentment. AN 8.54's balanced-finances duty and DN 31's duties of friendship
pull in opposite directions, which is the point. Expect the room to offer
"lend half" as a compromise; press on what half means as a message. The
scenario's honesty lies in what the canon leaves open: no contracts, no
interest, no procedure for non-repayment.

## s14 · The good job you'd hate · Career → session 3.5

Open on the dinner table: "what would you have to avoid saying about this
job?" That makes the cost social and specific, which is where right
livelihood actually bites. The trap is treating this as a debate between
idealism and rent — the canon ranks neither above the other. Use the five
forbidden trades as a test: if none applies, the question becomes what your
days are made of, and that is not a question the text answers for you. Give
the provider's position a full hearing; it is the one most students will
actually live by.

## Closing note for instructors

The two new scenarios are deliberately unresolved in different ways. s13
has a clear moral structure but no procedure; s14 has no clear moral
structure at all, only competing goods. Running them back-to-back after
Track 3 (s13) and Track 4 (s14) tests whether the path has taught students
to tell the two kinds of difficulty apart. If they cannot, revisit session
3.5's inference note before the seminar.
