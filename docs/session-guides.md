# Session Guides

One guide per session, for instructors and reading-group leaders. Each gives
the session's job, an opening move, the misreading to correct, and a move if
the room stalls. The 24 lab-scenario guides are separate (`guides.md`).

**Format.** 60–90 minutes. Participants read the session beforehand (Read and
Understand); the meeting starts with the passage, not the paraphrase. Close
the paraphrase while discussing. No preparation is required of participants
beyond the reading.

---

## Track 1 — How to Read This

### 1.1 Be Your Own Island · DN 16
**Job:** establish that the path is a practice, not a person.
**Open with:** “If the teacher is dying, what is left?” Collect answers before
reading.
**Misreading:** “Be your own island” means isolation or rejecting teachers.
The text says take the teaching as refuge and practise unattended.
**If stalled:** ask what the four fields (body, feelings, mind, principles)
would look like in one ordinary day.

### 1.2 The Arrow · MN 63
**Job:** teach that some questions are real but badly timed.
**Open with:** “Name a question you want answered before you will act.”
**Misreading:** the sutta is anti-intellectual, or says the questions are
meaningless. It says they are undeclared and cannot be a precondition.
**If stalled:** put the four truths on the board and ask which question the
arrow simile actually answers.

### 1.3 Four Great References · AN 4.180
**Job:** give the group a procedure for testing a claim.
**Open with:** “Where do you get your authority from — text, person, or
experience?” Write the three on the board.
**Misreading:** the sutta says “check against the canon” as a slogan; it
gives four references and a method.
**If stalled:** run a real claim through the test and let the group disagree
about what counts as “found in the discourses”.

### 1.4 Two Translations · Dhp 1–2
**Job:** make interpretation visible.
**Open with:** read the two renderings aloud without saying which is which;
ask what differs.
**Misreading:** one translation is “right”. The point is the choice.
**If stalled:** ask what “mind” (mano) includes that “heart” does not.

### 1.5 The Raft · MN 22
**Job:** distinguish using the teaching from clinging to it.
**Open with:** “What have you been carrying that you could put down?”
**Misreading:** the teaching is disposable, so nothing matters. Even good
teachings are for crossing; that is not contempt for them.
**If stalled:** ask what “carrying the raft on your head” looks like in study
practice — notes, terminology, identity.

### 1.6 The Kālāmas · AN 3.65
**Job:** replace “believe nothing” with a testable method.
**Open with:** “What would you need to see to change your mind about
something you believe?”
**Misreading:** the sutta licenses wholesale doubt. It rejects four grounds
and gives observed harm/benefit as the criterion.
**If stalled:** apply the test to a claim about work or money, not religion.

## Checkpoint 1 · Looking Back
**Job:** retrieval, not review. Do not reteach.
**Open with:** “Close the app. Three passages; answer before opening them.”
**Misreading:** the checkpoint is a test to pass. It is diagnostic.
**If stalled:** ask which passage was hardest to reconstruct and why; reread
only that session.

## Track 2 — What the Teaching Claims

### 2.1 The Claim Behind the Verses · Dhp 1–2
**Job:** turn a familiar verse into a testable causal claim.
**Open with:** “What does this verse predict will happen if you rehearse
resentment for a week?”
**Misreading:** it is a proverb about positivity. It claims intention
precedes and shapes experience.
**If stalled:** ask what evidence would count against the claim.

### 2.2 A Handful of Leaves · SN 56.31
**Job:** establish that the teaching is deliberately partial.
**Open with:** “What would you want a complete map of reality to include?”
**Misreading:** the omitted teachings are false, or the Buddha knew nothing
else. The claim is sufficiency for one goal.
**If stalled:** ask what the app itself leaves out, and whether that is
honest.

### 2.3 Intention Is the Act · AN 6.63
**Job:** make intention part of the deed, not preparation for it.
**Open with:** “Is a decision you never acted on an action?”
**Misreading:** “it is intention that I call deeds” means consequences do not
matter. It relocates the act; results still follow.
**If stalled:** audit a silence — who decided not to speak, and what was the
intention?

### 2.4 One Thing · SN 3.17
**Job:** show diligence as the condition for other goods.
**Open with:** “Which good intention of yours keeps slipping?”
**Misreading:** diligence means productivity or busyness. It means not
letting things slip.
**If stalled:** distinguish negligence from rest; ask what rest looks like
when it is chosen rather than drifted into.

### 2.5 Four Kinds of Happiness · AN 4.62
**Job:** rank ordinary goods without despising them.
**Open with:** “Rank these: money, enjoying it, being debt-free, a clear
conscience.”
**Misreading:** the sutta is anti-wealth. It names wealth as a real good and
ranks blamelessness above it.
**If stalled:** ask what can be taken from each good, and by whom.

### 2.6 The First Turning · SN 56.11
**Job:** read the four truths as diagnosis and treatment.
**Open with:** “If this were a medical consultation, what is the diagnosis?”
**Misreading:** the truths are gloomy propositions to believe. They are
tasks: understand, abandon, realise, develop.
**If stalled:** map each truth onto the three phases (knowing, doing, done).

## Track 3 — The Ethical Life

### 3.1 Fourteen Things Left Behind · DN 31
**Job:** show ethics beginning as subtraction.
**Open with:** “What would you have to stop doing for your work life to be
clean?”
**Misreading:** ethics is only avoidance. The fourteen are conditions for the
positive duties that follow.
**If stalled:** ask which of the six drains on wealth is most respectable in
your environment.

### 3.2 The Six Directions · DN 31
**Job:** make duty reciprocal.
**Open with:** “Pick a relationship. What do you owe, and what are you owed?”
**Misreading:** the sutta is a list of duties for subordinates. Every
direction has duties on both sides.
**If stalled:** read the worker/master pair aloud; ask which side is usually
omitted when the text is quoted.

### 3.3 What Counts as a Blessing · Snp 2.4
**Job:** replace luck with ordinary competence.
**Open with:** “What do people around you call a blessing?”
**Misreading:** the list is a prosperity gospel. It is decent living,
including work and family duties.
**If stalled:** ask which item on the list a modern self-help book would
leave out.

### 3.4 Eight Things a Household Needs · AN 8.54
**Job:** treat household welfare as a discipline.
**Open with:** “Which of the four — effort, protection, friendship, balance —
is weakest in your life?”
**Misreading:** the budget rule is prosperity advice. It is a balance rule
with a moral frame.
**If stalled:** ask what “protection” means when the threat is not theft.

### 3.5 Five Trades · AN 5.177
**Job:** refuse the idea that livelihood is neutral.
**Open with:** “Is your income ethically neutral? What would make it not?”
**Misreading:** the list is a complete code. It is five trades, and the text
addresses trade, not employment or investment.
**If stalled:** apply the narrow and broad readings to one industry and let
the group disagree.

### 3.6 How to Know a Person · AN 4.192
**Job:** slow down character judgement.
**Open with:** “How long have you actually known the people you trust?”
**Misreading:** the four tests are a personality quiz. They require time,
attention, and cost.
**If stalled:** rank the four by cost and ask which one people substitute for
the others.

## Checkpoint 2 · What You Actually Used
**Job:** separate understanding from use.
**Open with:** “Which of these have you used, and which did you only
understand?”
**Misreading:** a good answer is a summary of the session. The question is
about behaviour.
**If stalled:** ask for one concrete instance, not a general intention.

## Track 4 — The Mind

### 4.1 Two Kinds of Thought · MN 19
**Job:** make attention causal.
**Open with:** “What thought do you rehearse most, and where does it lead?”
**Misreading:** the sutta is about suppressing thoughts. It is about seeing
where they lead.
**If stalled:** use the cowherd image: what is the crop, and what is the
penalty for wandering?

### 4.2 Five Ways to Put a Thought Down · MN 20
**Job:** give a ladder of techniques ordered by cost.
**Open with:** “What do you do when a thought will not leave?”
**Misreading:** the last technique (crushing) is the model. It is the
emergency option.
**If stalled:** rank the five by how costly they are, then by how virtuous
they feel.

### 4.3 The Mirror · MN 61
**Job:** install the three-time check.
**Open with:** “Which action of yours would fail a mid-act check?”
**Misreading:** the check is a rule list. It is a harm question in three
tenses.
**If stalled:** run one real decision through before/during/after on paper.

### 4.4 Two Arrows · SN 36.6
**Job:** separate sensation from the story added to it.
**Open with:** “Describe a small pain, then describe the story about it.”
**Misreading:** the second arrow is optional, so the first is too. The
sensation remains; the reaction is what is trainable.
**If stalled:** ask what “resistance” looks like in the body before it looks
like a thought.

### 4.5 The Lute String · AN 6.55
**Job:** give permission to tune effort.
**Open with:** “Where are you over-tight right now?”
**Misreading:** the sutta says try less. It says balance, and it is addressed
to someone already straining.
**If stalled:** ask what “slack” would look like — and whether it is rest or
avoidance.

### 4.6 Breathing, Plainly · MN 118
**Job:** present attention as ordinary and trainable.
**Open with:** read the safety note aloud. Then: “What did you notice about
your breath just now?”
**Misreading:** this is meditation training. It is a reading of an
instruction, with a five-minute option.
**If stalled:** stop the exercise. Discuss why the text starts with noticing
heavy and light, not with concentration.

## Track 5 — The Deep End

### 5.1 Nothing You Can Point To · SN 22.59
**Job:** make the not-self argument, and prevent nihilism.
**Open with:** “Name something you can command to stay the same.”
**Misreading:** not-self means you do not exist, or nothing matters.
**If stalled:** return to the control test; ask what the text concludes —
disenchantment, not denial.

### 5.2 Everything Is Burning · SN 35.28
**Job:** locate the fire in the relation, not the world.
**Open with:** “What does ‘burning’ name here — the object or the contact?”
**Misreading:** the world is illusory. The fires are greed, hate, delusion.
**If stalled:** ask which sense door the group uses most, and what it costs.

### 5.3 The Chain You Can Watch · SN 12.2
**Job:** make conditionality observable.
**Open with:** “Trace a bad mood backwards: contact, feeling, demand.”
**Misreading:** the twelve links are a timeline. They are a structure with
joints.
**If stalled:** choose one link and ask what its definition includes that the
English word hides.

### 5.4 The Unborn · Ud 8.3
**Job:** respect the text's refusal to picture nibbāna.
**Open with:** “Write your picture of peace. Now delete it.”
**Misreading:** nibbāna is a place or a blissful state. The text describes by
negation and argument.
**If stalled:** ask what each group member's picture adds that the sutta does
not say.

### 5.5 Eight Practices, Not Eight Virtues · SN 45.8
**Job:** read the path as procedure.
**Open with:** “Define right speech without using the word ‘kind’.”
**Misreading:** the factors are character traits. They are defined by
refusals and knowings.
**If stalled:** score the eight 1–5 and ask which definition was hardest to
state.

### 5.6 The Questions Left Alone · MN 72
**Job:** make the refusal a method, not a dodge.
**Open with:** “What question do you demand an answer to?”
**Misreading:** the Buddha has a secret answer. The four categories do not
apply.
**If stalled:** use the fire simile; ask where an extinguished fire went.

## Track 6 — Living With Others

### 6.1 True, Useful, Timely · MN 58
**Job:** make truth insufficient on its own.
**Open with:** “Tell us a true thing you said that did not need saying.”
**Misreading:** the three tests are a green light. They are a brake as well.
**If stalled:** take a hard sentence and test it three ways; see which test it
fails.

### 6.2 Half Is Not Enough · SN 45.2
**Job:** treat company as constitutive.
**Open with:** “Who is actually good for you in this sense?”
**Misreading:** the sutta is about having friends. It is about the path being
carried by persons.
**If stalled:** ask whether the group can name anyone they are that for.

### 6.3 The Saw Simile · MN 21
**Job:** set the standard for speech under attack.
**Open with:** “What would ‘no bad words’ require in your hardest current
relationship?”
**Misreading:** the sutta requires staying in harm. It forbids retaliatory
corruption, not exit.
**If stalled:** distinguish endurance from passivity; ask what leaving would
look like.

### 6.4 Everyone Holds Themselves Dear · SN 3.8
**Job:** turn self-regard into an argument against harm.
**Open with:** “Where do you treat your self-regard as reasonable but someone
else's as selfish?”
**Misreading:** everyone is selfish, so I may be too. The conclusion is an
obligation.
**If stalled:** ask what changes if the person annoying you wants what you
want.

### 6.5 Five Things to Remember · AN 5.57
**Job:** clarity about mortality, without gloom.
**Open with:** read the safety note. Then: “Which vanity is making you
careless?”
**Misreading:** this is a depression exercise. It is a recollection aimed at
carelessness.
**If stalled:** stop. Ask what “ordinary” thing they will do after the minute
is over.

### 6.6 The Last Words · DN 16
**Job:** close the path where it opened.
**Open with:** “Write your own last line.”
**Misreading:** the ending is a doctrine. It is a statement about conditions
and an imperative.
**If stalled:** compare 1.1 and 6.6; ask what changed in the reader, not in
the text.

---

## Checkpoint 3 · The Whole Path, In Three Passages
**Job:** test defence, not recall.
**Open with:** “Which of these could you defend, and which would you only
repeat?”
**Misreading:** a correct summary is enough. The difference between defending
and repeating is the point.
**If stalled:** ask for the strongest objection to the passage they chose,
then whether they can answer it.
