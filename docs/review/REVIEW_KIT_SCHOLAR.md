# Scholar Review Kit — Level 1 Doctrine (5.1–5.6) and Level 2 (C, D)

**Generic packet. No reviewer is named.** Send this file, the app URL, and the
two listing documents (`docs/TRANSLATION_COVERAGE.md`, `docs/STYLE_GUIDE.md`).

---

## 1. What this is

SuttaPath teaches the Pali Canon in English. Canon text is Bhikkhu Sujato's
CC0 translation, quoted verbatim and attributed. Everything else — paraphrase,
context, inference, practice, sessions, scenarios — is ours.

We are asking for a scholarly check of the **editorial layer only**. You are
not reviewing the translation, the app design, or the prose style. You are
checking whether our commentary is faithful, labelled, and honest about what
the text does not settle.

**Time:** roughly 8–10 hours for the required scope (18 sessions), or 3–4
hours for Level 1 (5.1–5.6) alone.

## 2. The three questions, per session

For each session, answer:

1. **Accuracy.** Is every paraphrase a fair rendering of the cited segments?
   Flag any sentence that adds a claim the text does not make.
2. **Labelling.** Is every interpretive claim either attributed to us
   (“Our reading, not the text”) or clearly presented as the text's? Flag
   smuggling.
3. **Honesty of the gap.** Where the session says the canon leaves something
   open, is that true? Flag places where we have invented a gap, or closed
   one that is real.

You do **not** need to comment on: word choice, reading level, layout,
pedagogy, or the app.

## 3. Ground rules

- The canon layer is frozen. Please do not propose edits to quoted text.
- Where you disagree with our reading, say so and cite the segment. We will
  either change the inference or record the disagreement in the session.
- “This is defensible but not the only reading” is a useful verdict; use it.
- If a session is fine, say so. Silence is ambiguous.

## 4. Required scope and claim checklists

Segment IDs are SuttaCentral. Each session below lists the claims most worth
checking, not every sentence.

### Level 1 · 5.1 Nothing You Can Point To (SN 22.59)

- The sutta is traditionally the second sermon, addressed to the five
  ascetics. (Setting; check the traditional framing is not overstated.)
- The argument: if form were self, one could compel it; one cannot;
  therefore not-self. Same move for consciousness. (2.1–5.5)
- The interrogation adds impermanence → suffering → “not fit to be regarded
  as mine, I am this, my self”. (6.1–6.18)
- The application extends to all aggregates, past/future/internal/external.
  (7.1–10.1)
- Result: disillusionment → fading of desire → freedom. (11.1–11.3)
- **Our inference to check:** “The argument is about control and ownership,
  not existence”; “the text never says there is no person”; “disenchantment,
  not nihilism”.

### 5.2 Everything Is Burning (SN 35.28)

- Gayā Head, a thousand mendicants. The “former fire-worshippers” detail is
  traditional/commentarial, not in the sutta. We say “the tradition says”.
  Is that sufficient?
- “All is burning” is analysed through six senses, objects, consciousness,
  contact, feeling. (1.3–1.13)
- The fires are greed, hate, delusion. (1.7, 1.13)
- The response is disenchantment, fading, freedom. (1.14–1.17)
- **Our inference:** not “the world is illusory”; the burning is relational;
  nibbāna as extinguishment is a pun that is doing work.

### 5.3 The Chain You Can Watch (SN 12.2)

- The twelve links, stated in order. (2.2–2.13)
- The sutta then defines each link. (3.1 to end)
- **Our inference:** the turn at feeling is where practice can intervene; the
  chain is not a timeline; commentarial traditions differ on how literally to
  read links across lives. Please check the last point for overgeneralisation.
- We say memorising twelve items without locating a joint misses the point.
  Is that a fair characterisation of the text's purpose?

### 5.4 The Unborn (Ud 8.3)

- The text: freedom from rebirth, from the produced, made, conditioned; the
  conditional argument that if there were no such freedom there would be no
  escape. (3.1–3.3)
- **Our inference:** description by negation; traditions differ on what
  nibbāna is; any positive picture adds what the text withholds.
- Please flag if “the unconditioned is not simply the conditioned switched
  off” oversteps.

### 5.5 Eight Practices, Not Eight Virtues (SN 45.8)

- The eight factors. (2.1–2.2)
- Right view = knowledge of the four truths. (3.1–3.3)
- Right purpose = renunciation, good will, harmlessness. (4.1–4.2)
- Right speech = refraining from false, divisive, harsh, pointless speech.
  (5.2–5.3)
- Right livelihood = giving up wrong livelihood. (7.1–7.3)
- Right effort = four efforts. (8.2–8.5)
- **Our inference:** definitions are procedural; the ordering is a claim
  (view shapes purpose, etc.); the factors are a system, not a menu.

### 5.6 The Questions Left Alone (MN 72)

- Four positions about the realized one after death, all refused.
  (9.1–12.3, 16.1–16.8)
- The reason: these are a thicket of views, not leading to peace. (14.1)
- The fire simile: an extinguished fire has gone in no direction.
  (19.11–19.18) The freed one is “like the ocean”; the four positions do not
  apply. (20.1–20.4)
- **Our inference:** the refusal is methodological, not a secret answer;
  later traditions developed answers; we describe the sutta, not adjudicate.

### Level 2 · C1 (SN 22.59, whole) and C2 (SN 35.28, whole)

Same texts as 5.1 and 5.2, read whole. Check that the section notes and the
inference do not overreach, and that “the aggregate is a verb-like analysis”
(C1) is presented as our gloss, not the text's.

### C3 Conditionality, Both Directions (SN 12.2, whole)

- The chain and the definitions. (2.1 to end)
- **Our inference:** “empirical analysis”; each link a category of experience,
  not a substance; the definitions are the doctrine. Please flag if
  “empirical” is anachronistic or misleading.
- Note the absence of the reverse (cessation) sequence in SN 12.2; we do not
  claim it is there.

### C4 Kamma, Rebirth, and No-Self (AN 3.65 + MN 22)

This is the session most likely to need correction.

- Kamma: intentional action and its tendency to produce results; threefold
  ripening (this life, next, later).
- Rebirth is assumed in the canon, not argued in this session.
- Not-self is argued from control/impermanence (MN 22:25.1–25.5; SN 22.59).
- The four consolations (AN 3.65:43.1–46.1) are reasons for conduct under
  uncertainty, not a proof of rebirth.
- **Our inference:** the canon holds kamma, rebirth, and not-self without a
  unified metaphysics of continuity; later schools supplied theories.
  Please check this claim specifically — it is the load-bearing one.

### C5 Jhāna and the Formless (AN 5.28, whole)

- Five factors = four absorptions + the basis for reviewing. (1.1–6.4)
- The similes (bath powder, spring-fed lake, lotuses, white cloth).
- The capabilities listed afterwards. (7.1–16.2)
- **Our framing:** a map of attainments, not instructions. Is that adequate
  and accurate?
- “Pervading the body” — check our wording against the similes.

### C6 From Jhāna to Awakening (MN 36, excerpts)

- The black plum tree memory; the first absorption; feeding the body; the
  three knowledges. (31.1–31.5, 34.1–34.2, 38.1–39.2, 42.1–44.1)
- **Our inference:** jhāna is a condition for the knowledges, not a
  substitute. Check the narrative sequencing.
- We use “rose-apple” nowhere now; the translation says black plum.

### D1 One Teaching, Two Nikāyas (MN 10 vs DN 22)

- The refrain is effectively identical. (mn10:5.1–5.3; dn22:2.11–2.13)
- DN 22 is longer and expands the fourth foundation into the four truths.
- **Our inference:** differences show editorial work in transmission; the
  comparative question is what each version does, not which is original.
- Please check “roughly half again as long” against the word counts in
  `TRANSLATION_COVERAGE.md`.

### D2 Two Arguments for One Doctrine (SN 12.2 vs DN 15)

- SN 12.2 is declarative; DN 15 interrogates each link with “is there a
  specific condition?” and a counterfactual. (dn15:2.1–2.8)
- **Our inference:** genre is part of the doctrine; a list can be memorised,
  an argument must be understood.

### D3 Translation as Evidence (SN 36.6)

- dukkha: literal “bad axle-hole; hard to bear”; rendered “suffering”.
- vedanā: affective tone (pleasant/painful/neutral), not emotion.
- sati: literal “remembering, keeping in mind”; rendered “mindfulness”.
- **Check:** the claim that “mindfulness” as a translation is about a century
  old and now functions as a brand. Flag if the history is too compressed.

### D4 Pali Tools I (Dhp 1)

- anattā = an- + attā; the gloss is a negation, not “no soul”.
- Method claims only: dictionary gives range, passage gives the sense,
  concordance gives usage. We make no parsing claims beyond the glossary.

### D5 Pali Tools II (MN 10)

- sati / sampajañña: keeping in mind / clear knowing.
- vitakka / vicāra: initial movement of attention / continued application.
- saṅkhāra: translated differently by context (choices, formations,
  conditions).
- **These are the Level 2 claims most in need of a Pali reader.** Please flag
  any that a specialist would reject outright.

### D6 Commentary and the Sutta (MN 10)

- The sutta's hindrance instruction is presence, arising, abandonment,
  non-arising. (36.1–36.4)
- Everything about proximate causes, counteractive factors, and absorption
  mapping is commentarial, not sutta.
- **Our framing:** label the layers; do not let commentary impersonate the
  text. No commentarial text is quoted.

4. **5.2** — the former-fire-worshipper tradition.
5. **D3** — the history of “mindfulness”.

## 6. How to report

Use this form per session (copy into a document or email):

```
Session: 5.1 / C4 / ...
Verdict: PASS | PASS WITH MINOR CHANGES | REVISE
Accuracy issues (quote our sentence + segment):
Labelling issues:

```

For a revision, one or two sentences are enough. We will make the change and
record it; you will not be asked to re-review the whole session unless you ask
to.
