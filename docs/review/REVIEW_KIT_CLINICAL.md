# Clinical / Safety Review Kit — meditation-adjacent content

**Generic packet. No reviewer is named.**

Send this file, the app URL, and the four sessions below. The review is of
**safety framing**, not of doctrine or design.

---

## 1. What the app is, and is not

SuttaPath is a reading course. It is not therapy, not a meditation app, and
not a retreat manual. It teaches what texts say; it does not prescribe
attainment. Every practice suggestion is small and optional, and every
session can be marked complete without doing it.

The content that needs your eye is where the canon touches breath, mortality,
grief, and altered states. We have written stop-instructions; we want to know
whether they are adequate, and whether any framing inadvertently encourages
practice it should not.

**Time:** 60–90 minutes.

## 2. Scope

| Session | What it contains | Current framing |
|---|---|---|
| **4.6 Breathing, Plainly** (MN 118, excerpts) | Four breath steps; five-minute practice | “Before you start” note: breath practice can surface difficult material, especially with trauma or panic; stop if it destabilises you; skip the session; crisis line |
| **6.5 Five Things to Remember** (AN 5.57) | Ageing, illness, death, separation, ownership of deeds | Note: clarity not gloom; stop and skip if it darkens your mood; grief/unwell → come back later. Practice: one sentence, one minute, then stop |
| **B1 MN 10** (whole text) | Charnel-ground contemplations on decay | Note: intense by design; skip that section if it destabilises you; “nothing here is a meditation assignment” |
| **B6 MN 118** (whole text) | Sixteen steps, four tetrads, jhāna factors | Note: description, not a retreat manual; stop if it destabilises you; later sections are claims, not targets. Live practice: “take one tetrad for one sitting” |
| **C5 Jhāna and the Formless** (AN 5.28) | Four absorptions and the reviewing basis; the powers list | Note: advanced attainments described, not prescribed; similes are not techniques; “map, not a practice assignment” |

## 3. Questions we need answered

For each session, and then overall:

1. **Stop language.** Is “stop if this destabilises you” specific enough to
   act on? Would you add an instruction to ground, open the eyes, or contact
   someone? Give exact wording if so.
2. **Eligibility.** Should the app say plainly that this is not suitable for
   someone in acute crisis, acute grief, or with a trauma history without
   support? We currently say “skip” and “come back later”; is that enough?
3. **Crisis signposting.** “Contact your local emergency number or a crisis
   line” avoids region-specific numbers. Is that adequate, or should there be
   a link/resource policy?
4. **Breath practice.** 4.6 offers a five-minute exercise; B6 asks the reader
   to take one tetrad for one sitting. Does that cross from “reading about a
   practice” into “instruction”? If it does, what would keep it on the
   reading side — removing the exercise, changing the wording, adding a
   check?
5. **Mortality content.** B1's charnel contemplations and 6.5's recollections
   are intense. Is a note and a skip option sufficient, or should there be an
   explicit opt-in, an age note, or a stronger warning?
6. **Attainment content.** C5 lists jhāna and the powers. Does “map, not
   assignment” hold, given the surrounding similes?
7. **Benefit claims.** Scan for anything that promises calm, healing, or
   relief. Flag it. (“Steadying” in 6.5 is the kind of word we mean.)
8. **Anything else** you would not want a vulnerable reader to meet
   unaccompanied.

## 4. What is out of scope

- Doctrinal accuracy (separate scholar review).
- Translation choices.
- App design, data handling, or progress features. (No accounts; notes and
  progress stay in the browser. No analytics.)
- Whether the canon “really” teaches these practices.

## 5. Our own concerns

Listed so you can disagree:

- “Stop if this destabilises you” may be too abstract for someone already
  destabilised.
- 6.5's “if it darkens your mood” relies on self-monitoring that depressed
  readers may not do reliably.
- B6's Live practice asks for a sitting; the safety note is on the Read tab
  only (the note appears above the session, so it is visible in every mode,
  but the practice is in Live).
- No resource list is provided for any region.
- The charnel-ground section is opt-out, not opt-in.
