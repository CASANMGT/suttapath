# SuttaPath — The Path
**36 sessions · 6 tracks · 24 integrity-lab scenarios**

---

## 0. How to read this document

Each session is defined by **one primary text**, **one idea**, and **one practice**. Nothing else is allowed in a session.

```
Session 3.1  ·  12 min  ·  DN 31 (excerpt)
Text    Sigālovāda Sutta — the layperson's code
Idea    Ethics is not a list of rules but a set of relationships you maintain.
Live    Pick one relationship from the six. What does it actually owe?
Terms   sīla · kalyāṇamitta · apāyamukha
Lab     #5 The exam you could pass
```

**On citations.** Every reference here is given from working knowledge and is *deliberately* unverified. Before any editorial copy is written, run a citation pass against a pinned SuttaCentral snapshot and correct anything that drifted. The build must fail on an unresolvable reference (`CONTENT_SCHEMA.md` §5). Do not skip this — a study app with wrong citations is worse than no app.

**On excerpting.** The canon layer stores each sutta complete. A session selects a *contiguous range* of segments. Never splice. If the relevant passage is eight segments long, the session is eight segments long.

**On difficulty.** The path is deliberately front-loaded with *how to read*, not with doctrine. A learner who does Track 1 can read the rest. A learner who starts at the Four Noble Truths without it will read a summary of Buddhism they have seen before.

## 1. Track map

| # | Track | Register | Ends with |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **How to Read This** | Meta — no doctrine yet | You can read a sutta unaided |
| 2 | **What the Teaching Claims** | Foundational doctrine, lightly | The four truths as a structure |
| 3 | **The Ethical Life** | Householder ethics | You have a working moral vocabulary |
| 4 | **The Mind** | Attention, habit, emotion | A daily practice you've actually done |
| 5 | **The Deep End** | Not-self, conditionality, the unknown | You can hold what the canon won't answer |
| 6 | **Living With Others** | Speech, friendship, death | The canon's last words |

**Arc:** *learn to read → learn what it says → learn how to act → learn how to attend → learn what it means → learn how to be with people and die.* That is also, roughly, the canon's own sequence.

**Checkpoints** after Tracks 2, 4, and 6 — no new text, three cold re-readings, one question. See §7.

**Pacing:** 2 sessions/week ≈ 4.5 months. Sessions are 8–15 minutes. Track 1 can be done in one sitting.

## 2. Track 1 — How to Read This
*Six sessions. No doctrine. The goal is that by 1.6 the learner can open any sutta and know what kind of object they are holding.*

| # | Source | One idea | Live practice |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | **DN 16** §2.26 (final journey, "be your own island") | This is a library of training instructions, not a book of revelations. Nobody is going to save you; the text is a map. | Write one sentence: what do you actually want from this? |
| 1.2 | **MN 63** Cūḷamālukya (the arrow) | A sutta answers a question someone actually asked. Find the question and the text opens. | Name a question you're carrying that this path will *not* answer. |
| 1.3 | **AN 4.180** Mahāpadesa (four great references) | The tradition itself commands you to check teachings against the source rather than trust a teacher. | Where do you usually get your authority from? |
| 1.4 | **Dhp 1–2** (two translations side by side) | Translation is interpretation. *Manopubbaṅgamā dhammā* is not settled by any one English rendering. | Pick the rendering you'd defend, and say why. |
| 1.5 | **MN 22** (raft simile) | Even the teaching is for crossing, not for carrying. It is a tool that must eventually be put down. | What have you held onto that had stopped being useful? |
| 1.6 | **AN 3.65** Kālāma Sutta | Don't accept a teaching because of tradition, scripture, logic, or the teacher's charisma. Test it against observed harm. | Pick one belief in this list. What would count as evidence against it? |

**Scaffolding notes**
- 1.1 is the only session with no practice-text tension; it carries the orientation: what the Tipiṭaka is (three baskets, five nikāyas, ~40 volumes), who wrote it down and when, why "Pali Canon" means Theravāda, and the honesty note about Mahāyāna and Vajrayāna.
- 1.4 is the session that teaches the app's own epistemics: this app's paraphrase is one reading among several, and it says so.
- 1.2 must teach the **anatomy of a sutta** explicitly: setting → interlocutor → question → teaching → simile → response. Every later session refers back to this.

**Terms:** *sutta, nikāya, tipiṭaka, dhamma, saṅgha, buddha.*

## 3. Track 2 — What the Teaching Claims
*Six sessions. The frame of the whole thing, before the ethics.*

| # | Source | One idea | Live practice |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | **Dhp 1–2** (revisit, now as doctrine) | Mind precedes action. What you dwell on becomes the shape of your life. | Note one thought you returned to repeatedly today. |
| 2.2 | **SN 56.31** Siṃsapā (handful of leaves) | The Buddha explicitly declined to teach everything. The scope is what serves liberation, not what satisfies curiosity. | What do you want to know that wouldn't change how you live? |
| 2.3 | **AN 6.63** Nibbedhika ("intention is what I call action") | Kamma is not fate and not a cosmic ledger. It is the weight of intention behind what you do. | Recall one action today. What was the intention behind it? |
| 2.4 | **SN 3.17** Appamāda (diligence) | Nothing in this path happens by accident. The one quality the Buddha praised above all was not giving up. | What is one small thing you will not let slide this week? |
| 2.5 | **AN 4.62** Aṇaṇa (the four happinesses) | The canon is not anti-pleasure. It names four ordinary happinesses, and ranks a clear conscience first. | Which of the four did you have today? |
| 2.6 | **SN 56.11** Dhammacakkappavattana | The four truths are a diagnosis, not a creed: *what's wrong, why, that it can stop, how.* | Rewrite the four in your own words, no Pali. |

**Scaffolding notes**
- 2.5 is the corrective to the assumption that Buddhism is life-denying. Four happinesses: ownership (*atthi*), enjoyment (*bhoga*), debtlessness (*aṇaṇa*), blamelessness (*anavajja*).
- 2.6 is the spine of the whole canon. The Understand layer needs a diagram: diagnosis → cause → prognosis → treatment, and the three-turnings structure (know the truth, know the task, know it's done).
- 2.3 must confront the modern misreading of kamma directly. Say what it is not.

**Terms:** *dukkha, taṇhā, kamma, cetanā, appamāda, ariya-sacca, magga.*

## 4. Track 3 — The Ethical Life
*Six sessions. The most immediately useful track, and the one that earns the university audience.*

| # | Source | One idea | Live practice |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | **DN 31** (opening: four defilements, six ways to lose wealth) | Most harm is not dramatic. It is drift: intoxicants, late nights, festivals, gambling, bad friends, idleness. | Which of the six is closest to you? No answer required. |
| 3.2 | **DN 31** (the six directions) | Ethics is not a rule list; it is six relationships, each with reciprocal duties. | Pick one direction. Write what each side owes the other. |
| 3.3 | **Sn 2.4** Maṅgala Sutta | A "blessing" in the canon is not luck. It is a list of unglamorous competences. | Which blessing are you actually working on? |
| 3.4 | **AN 8.54** Dīghajāṇu (welfare here and now) | Four practical conditions for a good life: diligence, protection, good friends, balanced living. Not wealth maximisation. | Which of the four is weakest in your life? |
| 3.5 | **AN 5.177** Vaṇijjā (five trades) | Right livelihood is not "a job you like." It is refusing to earn from weapons, beings, meat, liquor, poison. | Where does your income sit on that list? What would you refuse? |
| 3.6 | **AN 4.192** (four ways to know a person) | Character is revealed by cohabitation, dealings, adversity, and conversation — in that order. | Which of the four have you actually tested someone with? |

**Scaffolding notes**
- 3.1 and 3.2 are the same sutta split deliberately. The first is diagnostic and uncomfortable; the second is constructive. Splitting prevents the "list of rules" reading.
- 3.2 is the single best session for the university audience. The six directions: parents, teachers, partner, friends, employees, spiritual guides. Each has duties *in both directions* — the canon is unusual in making obligations reciprocal.
- 3.5 must be honest: the five trades list is Iron-Age Indian, and modern application is contested. Panel 2 of the lab exists for exactly this.
- 3.3 must defuse the "blessing" frame for secular readers.

**Terms:** *sīla, apāyamukha, maṅgala, samajīvitā, sammā-ājīva, kalyāṇamitta.*

## 5. Track 4 — The Mind
*Six sessions. The track with a safety obligation attached.*

| # | Source | One idea | Live practice |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | **MN 19** Dvedhāvitakka | "Whatever you think about much becomes the inclination of your mind." Attention is not neutral. | For one day, name a thought as *leading somewhere* or *leading nowhere*. |
| 4.2 | **MN 20** Vitakkasaṇṭhāna | Five escalating techniques for a thought you can't drop: replace, examine the cost, ignore, take it apart, refuse it flatly. | Try the cheapest one first, in order, on a real rumination. |
| 4.3 | **MN 61** Ambalaṭṭhikā (the mirror) | Before, during, and after an action: does this lead to harm for me, for others, for both? | Run one decision through the three-time check. |
| 4.4 | **SN 36.6** Sallatha (two arrows) | Pain is one arrow. The resistance, story, and dread around it is the second, and it is optional. | Separate one discomfort today into arrow one and arrow two. |
| 4.5 | **AN 6.55** Soṇa (the lute) | Effort has a tuning. Too slack and you don't progress; too tight and you snap. | Where are you over-tight right now? |
| 4.6 | **MN 118** Ānāpānasati (excerpt) | Attention to breathing is trainable, ordinary, and unglamorous. | Five minutes, once. Not more. |

**Scaffolding notes**
- **Safety:** 4.6 must open with a grounding note. Breath practice can surface difficult material, particularly for people with trauma histories or panic disorder. Non-intensive framing, an explicit "stop if this destabilises you," and a link to support. Do not market this track as meditation training.
- 4.2 is the canon's cognitive-behavioural session; it reads as startlingly modern and is a strong retention hook.
- 4.4 needs the two-arrows diagram. It is the most immediately useful idea in the entire path for a stressed student.
- 4.5 prevents the burnout that kills adult learning. Frame it as a permission slip.

**Terms:** *vedanā, saṅkhāra, sati, sampajañña, ānāpānasati, samatha, vipassanā, jhāna.*

## 6. Track 5 — The Deep End
*Six sessions. Hard. Placed here on purpose, after the learner has a working vocabulary and evidence that the thing is useful.*

| # | Source | One idea | Live practice |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5.1 | **SN 22.59** Anattalakkhaṇa | Not-self is not "you don't exist." It is: nothing you can point to is worth calling *yours* in the way you assume. | Try to find the thing you call "me" that isn't changing. |
| 5.2 | **SN 35.28** Ādittapariyāya (fire) | Everything you experience is burning with craving, aversion, and confusion. That changes what "enjoying" means. | Notice one thing you enjoyed today. What was it actually doing? |
| 5.3 | **SN 12.2** Paṭiccasamuppāda (analysis) | Suffering is manufactured, in a chain you can observe, not inflicted. | Trace one bad mood backwards to what fed it. |
| 5.4 | **Ud 8.3** (the unborn) | Nibbāna is described by negation because it is not a place or a state you can picture. That is the point. | Write down your own picture of "being at peace." Then delete it. |
| 5.5 | **SN 45.8** (the eightfold path, analysed) | The path is not eight virtues. It is eight *practices*, and they are defined very concretely. | Score yourself 1–5 on each. No fixing yet. |
| 5.6 | **MN 72** Vacchagotta (the undeclared questions) | The canon refuses to answer some questions — and the refusal is a teaching, not a dodge. | Name one question you'll stop demanding an answer to. |

**Scaffolding notes**
- This track is where learners quit. The Understand layer must do more work here than anywhere: structural diagrams for 5.3 and 5.5, and a plain statement that 5.1 is often misunderstood.
- 5.2 needs historical framing: the Fire Sermon was delivered to former fire-worshippers, and the metaphor is doing something specific.
- 5.4 is the hardest editorial problem in the app. Do not explain nibbāna into a spa. Negative description, honest uncertainty, and a note that traditions differ.
- 5.6 is the ethical centre of the whole product. Explicit lesson: a system that knows its limits is more trustworthy than one that doesn't. For the university audience, this is the session to cite in an essay.

**Terms:** *anattā, anicca, khandha, upādāna, paṭiccasamuppāda, nibbāna, ariyasacca, avijjā.*

## 7. Track 6 — Living With Others
*Six sessions. Speech, friendship, anger, other people's minds, death, and the last words.*

| # | Source | One idea | Live practice |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6.1 | **MN 58** Abhayarājakumāra (speech) | Say it only if it is true, beneficial, and timely — and even then, gently. Truth alone is not a licence. | Catch one true thing you said that didn't need saying. |
| 6.2 | **SN 45.2** Upaḍḍha ("good friendship is the whole of the path") | You cannot do this alone, and the company you keep is not a detail. | Name the person who is actually good for you. Tell them. |
| 6.3 | **MN 21** Kakacūpama (the saw) | The standard is absurd and deliberate: even under extreme provocation, speech stays uncorrupted. | Choose one provocation this week and practise not retaliating. |
| 6.4 | **SN 3.8** Mallikā (self and other) | "Everyone holds themselves dearest." That is the basis for not harming, not a reason to be selfish. | Use the reflection: this person wants what I want. |
| 6.5 | **AN 5.57** (five recollections) | You will age, sicken, die, lose everyone, and inherit the results of your actions. Remembering this is clarifying, not morbid. | Say one of the five plainly. Sit with it for a minute. |
| 6.6 | **DN 16** (final words) — *bookends 1.1* | "All conditioned things decay. Strive with diligence." Then the app ends. | Write your own last line. |

**Scaffolding notes**
- 6.6 returns to the sutta that opened the path. Point this out explicitly — it is the design's only sentimental gesture and it is earned.
- 6.4 must prevent the misreading "everyone is selfish, so I should be too." The appeal is analogical, and the canon uses it as an argument against harm.
- 6.5 is the session most likely to be misread as depression. Frame: this is a clarity practice, not a gloom practice; if it darkens your mood, stop.

**Terms:** *vācā, kalyāṇamitta, mettā, khanti, maraṇānussati, appamāda.*

## 8. Checkpoints

Three sessions that contain no new text. Each is 10 minutes: three earlier passages shown cold, one question, no scoring.

| After | Passages shown | The question |
|---|---|---|
| Track 2 | 1.5 (raft), 2.1 (mind first), 2.6 (four truths) | "What would you have got wrong about this three weeks ago?" |
| Track 4 | 2.3 (intention), 3.2 (six directions), 4.4 (two arrows) | "Which of these have you actually used, and which did you only understand?" |
| Track 6 | 1.1 (island), 5.4 (the unborn), 6.6 (last words) | "Has your first answer to 1.1 changed? Say how." |

Checkpoints re-surface the *original translation* only — paraphrase is disabled. The point is to prove that the primary text has become readable.

## 11. Authoring rules for the content team

1. **One idea per session.** If the Understand panel needs the word "also" more than once, the session is too big.
2. **The paraphrase never replaces the translation.** It sits beneath it, visually subordinate, and is switched off by default.
3. **Every claim gets a segment UID.** Editorial prose that asserts what a text says must cite the segment.
4. **Flag inferences.** Use an explicit marker in the MDX: `[inference:]`. It renders differently from quotation — this is a hard requirement, not a style preference.
5. **Name the gap.** Every scenario and every hard session must have at least one sentence beginning "The canon does not address…" or "This assumes…".
6. **No second-person commands in the teaching voice.** "You should" is out. "The text says" and "a reader might" are in.
7. **No attainment claims.** Never imply that a practice produces a state, only that it is a practice.
8. **Reading level:** target grade 9–10 Flesch-Kincaid for Understand panels, grade 12 for the session intro. The translation itself is out of scope — do not simplify it.
9. **Length:** context 60–120 words; hook ≤ 20 words; one idea ≤ 25 words; practice ≤ 40 words.
10. **Every session ends with a way out.** A "stop here, that's enough" affordance. Adult learners need permission, and the canon's own advice on effort (4.5) supports it.
