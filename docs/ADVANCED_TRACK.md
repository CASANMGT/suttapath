# SuttaPath Level 2 — Reading the Canon

**A plan for the advanced tier: from guided excerpts to reading whole suttas,
comparing versions, and using Pali as a tool.**

Companion to `CURRICULUM.md` (Level 1: 36 sessions) and `CONTENT_PLAN.md`
(production phasing). This document is the design for what comes after a
reader finishes the beginner path.

---

## 1. Who this is for

Three overlapping readers, all of whom have finished Level 1 or its
equivalent:

- **The graduate.** Has read all 36 sessions, can state the one idea of a
  passage, and wants to stop needing the paraphrase.
- **The university student.** Needs to read a sutta in full, cite segments,
  and say something defensible about a translation choice.
- **The reading-group leader.** Wants to run a text with others and needs
  structure, not just enthusiasm.

**Entry is earned, not bought.** Level 1 works because it withholds. Level 2
only works if the reader can already do three things: state what a passage
claims, say what it does not claim, and find their way around a sutta's
numbering. A short entry check (read MN 63 unaided, answer three questions)
is enough; no account or score is needed.

---

## 2. The design shift

Level 1 is excerpt-based, one idea per session, 150–400 words of translation.
That is the right shape for orientation and wrong for depth. Level 2 changes
five things:

| Level 1 | Level 2 |
|---|---|
| One excerpt, one idea | One **whole text**, read with a structure map |
| Paraphrase under each passage | **Marginal notes** keyed to segment IDs; paraphrase only where needed |
| A single translation | **Comparison**: same passage in two texts or two renderings |
| Pali as glossary entries | Pali as a **tool**: parse a compound, use a dictionary, spot a translation choice |
| Mark complete | **Produce something**: a reading map, a 400-word note, an essay |

The constant is the layer rule: canon text verbatim, our commentary labelled,
and every claim traceable to a segment.

---

## 3. Structure

**Level 2 = 4 tracks × 6 sessions = 24 sessions.** Sessions run 25–60
minutes, because whole texts are longer. Each track ends with a checkpoint,
and the level ends with a capstone that is not a quiz.

### Track A — How the Canon Is Built (6 × 15–25 min)

The apparatus that makes independent reading possible. No new doctrine.

| # | Title | Sources | What the reader can do afterwards |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | The four nikāyas, and how to find a text | DN/SN/MN/AN structure; SuttaCentral navigation | Locate a sutta by collection, number, and title; know why the same text has several IDs |
| A2 | Formula, repetition, and the ellipsis | DN 16 refrain; MN 118 refrain | Read a repeated formula as structure, not padding; expand "…" correctly |
| A3 | Three reading protocols | SN 3.8 (dialogue), Snp 2.4 (verse), AN 4.41 (list) | Choose a protocol: follow an argument, follow an image, follow a list |
| A4 | The Khuddaka and the verse books | Dhp, Snp, Ud, Iti, Thag, Thig (orientation) | Know what the verse collections are for, and what they are not |
| A5 | Vinaya orientation | Vinaya excerpts, Brahmali CC0 | Describe what the monastic code covers and why lay readers should know its shape |
| A6 | Abhidhamma and commentary orientation | Orientation + short labelled excerpts | Distinguish sutta, abhidhamma, and aṭṭhakathā; know why this path does not major in them |

### Track B — Reading Whole Suttas (6 × 35–60 min)

One whole text per session, with a reading map and stop points. Verified
lengths are from the pinned-candidate snapshot (Sujato, CC0):

| # | Text | Words | The reading problem it teaches |
|---|---|---:|---|
| B1 | **MN 10** Satipaṭṭhāna | 3,143 | A long list sutta: find the refrain, see the fourfold structure, survive the repetition |
| B2 | **MN 39** Mahā-Assapura | 2,911 | The gradual training as a whole: a sutta that is itself a curriculum |
| B3 | **SN 12.23** Upanisa | 694 | Conditionality in both directions; short enough to read twice in one sitting |
| B4 | **AN 4.41** Samādhi-bhāvanā | 479 | A compact list sutta: four ways to develop concentration, no narrative |
| B5 | **DN 15** Mahānidāna | 3,860 | Dependent origination, self, and becoming — the hardest text in the level; read with a map, not unaided |
| B6 | **MN 118** (complete) | 1,934 | The sixteen steps end to end; the safety framing from Level 1 carries over |

Optional substitutions if a text proves too heavy in pilot: MN 38 (4,198) for
B5, DN 22 (4,920) moved to Track D as the comparison text.

### Track C — Doctrine in Depth (6 × 20–35 min)

Not new topics so much as the same topics with their joints exposed.

| # | Title | Sources | Depth added |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | The aggregates as a process | SN 22.59 + the fivefold scheme | Why "aggregate" is a verb-like analysis, not a list of parts |
| C2 | Sense bases, elements, and the sixfold world | SN 35.28 + SN 12.2 definitions | How experience is partitioned; what the partition is for |
| C3 | Conditionality, both directions | SN 12.2 + SN 12.23 | Origination and cessation; why "dependent" is not "determined" |
| C4 | Kamma, rebirth, and no-self | AN 3.65 + MN 22 | The hardest coherence problem in the canon, stated honestly |
| C5 | Jhāna and the formless | AN 5.28 + MN 36 (excerpt) | The similes, the sequence, and what the texts do not promise |
| C6 | From jhāna to awakening | MN 36 (excerpt: the discovery and the three knowledges) | How the path's goal is described in the Buddha's own account |

### Track D — Comparative and Critical Reading (6 × 30–45 min)

| # | Title | Sources | Skill |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | One teaching, two nikāyas | MN 10 vs DN 22 | Compare structure and refrain; explain why versions differ |
| D2 | Two arguments for one doctrine | SN 12.2 vs DN 15 | Analysis versus narrative as teaching strategies |
| D3 | Translation as evidence | One passage, public-domain + CC0 renderings | Separate what the text says from what the translator chose |
| D4 | Pali tools I | Word lab: dukkha, anattā, saṅkhāra | Parse a compound; use a dictionary; recognise a declension |
| D5 | Pali tools II | Word lab: sati/sampajañña, vitakka/vicāra | See how term clusters shape doctrine — and translations |
| D6 | Commentary and the sutta | Visuddhimagga on satipaṭṭhāna (orientation + short labelled excerpts) | Distinguish commentarial reading from the text; know what the commentary adds |

**Licensing rule for the comparison track.** Only CC0 or public-domain
translations may be reproduced verbatim. Where a modern translation is
copyrighted, the session links out and compares *our description* of the
choice, never the text. This is a hard rule, not a preference.

### Capstone — the unseen sutta

The level ends with a text the reader has not seen (e.g. AN 4.41 if it was
not used, or SN 12.23's neighbour). Unaided, in one sitting:

1. Read the whole sutta.
2. Write a 400-word note: what it claims, what it does not claim, one segment
   citation per claim, one translation choice worth noticing.
3. Compare against a model note, which shows its own citations and its own
   uncertainties.

This is the real assessment. It cannot be gamed by remembering paraphrase,
and it is the first thing in the product that resembles scholarship.

## 4. Level 3 — Seminar (optional, 6 workshops)

For readers who want to teach. Output, not consumption:

- Facilitate one lab scenario and write the debrief.
- Produce a six-week reading-group plan for a single sutta.
- Write a 1,500-word essay with segment citations, reviewed once.
- Record a ten-minute talk on one passage and transcribe the citations.
- Peer-review two other readers' notes against the layer rule.
- Run the capstone for someone else and mark only the citations.

No new canon content. Level 3 is where the app stops being a course and
becomes a workshop.

## 5. What this requires from the product

### Content schema additions

- `session.mode: "whole"` with `sections: [{ label, uids, note?, stop? }]`.
  The build checks that the ranges cover every non-empty segment of the sutta
  (or that omissions are declared).
- `session.compare: [{ label, uids }]` rendered as two aligned columns with a
  shared segment anchor.
- `paliLab: { term, forms: [{ form, parse, gloss }], note }` — every parse
  carries `reviewedBy` (a Pali specialist), because plausible-looking parses
  are worse than none.
- `spacedReview`: saved segments resurface at 3, 10, and 30 days, drawn from
  the reader's own completed sessions. Never scored.
- `capstone`: prompt, model note, citation requirements.

### App changes

- A long-read renderer with sticky section navigation, per-section notes, and
  "stop here" points.
- A compare view that highlights the same refrain in two texts.
- Segment-level deep links (`#/s/mn10#mn10:3.2`) so notes and essays can cite.
- A review queue screen, distinct from the path.
- Print: whole-text sessions print as a reading booklet, notes in the margin
  or as endnotes (choose one; test on paper).

### Canon snapshot

Adds roughly 12 texts (~30,000 words), including MN 10, DN 15, MN 39, MN 38,
DN 22, AN 5.28, MN 36, AN 8.30. The ingest already handles any Sujato UID;
the offline cache grows to a few megabytes, still trivial for a PWA.

### New build invariants

- Whole-text ranges cover all content-bearing segments, or declare the gaps.
- Compare passages are contiguous and each side is taught by the session.
- Every Pali parse has a named reviewer and a dictionary source.
- Capstone model notes cite only existing segments.

## 6. Outcomes (what "finished Level 2" means)

A reader should be able to:

1. Pick up an unseen sutta from the four nikāyas and read it with a map.
2. State its claim, its limits, and one thing it refuses to settle.
3. Cite it by segment, not by vibe.
4. Compare two versions and say what changed and why it matters.
5. Use a Pali dictionary on a key term without pretending to know the
   language.
6. Tell a sutta from a commentarial reading, and say which one they are
   doing.

That is the difference between having read about the canon and being able to
read it.

## 7. Effort and phasing

| Work | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Track A (6 sessions, apparatus) | 10 editor-days |
| Track B (6 whole texts, maps, notes) | 24 editor-days |
| Track C (6 doctrinal sessions) | 15 editor-days |
| Track D (6 comparative sessions + licensing review) | 18 editor-days |
| Pali strand (specialist) | 10 specialist-days |
| Capstone + model notes | 5 editor-days |
| Scholar review (whole level) | 10 reviewer-days |
| Engineering (long-read, compare, review queue, deep links, print) | 15–20 dev-days |

**≈ 110–120 working days**, roughly four months for one editor with a Pali
specialist and a scholar on call. Phase it:

- **2.0 Pilot** — Track A plus two whole-text sessions (MN 10, SN 12.23),
  new renderer, entry check. Gate: ten Level-1 graduates read MN 10 unaided,
  and at least six can state its structure without the map.
- **2.1 Depth** — Tracks B and C complete; Pali labs; compare view; spaced
  review.
- **2.2 Critical** — Track D, capstone, licensing and scholar review.
- **3.0 Seminar** — only if readers ask for it. Do not build it speculatively.

## 8. Risks, and what not to add

| Risk | Why it bites | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Length overwhelms** | 3,000–5,000 words is a different reading act | Section maps, stop points, two-sitting option, no paraphrase crutch |
| **Pali becomes a vanity course** | A little grammar is worse than none | Tool-based word labs only; specialist review; no declension drills |
| **Commentary smuggled in as text** | The tradition's readings are not the sutta's | Layer labels, explicit "commentary says" framing, review |
| **Licensing breach in comparisons** | Modern translations are copyrighted | CC0/public domain verbatim only; link out otherwise |
| **Meditation depth becomes instruction** | Jhāna and formless content invites attainment claims | Non-attainment framing, no practice targets, safety review |
| **Assessment creep** | Scores turn study into performance | Capstone is self-checked against a model note; nothing is stored as a grade |
| **Sectarian flattening** | "Buddhism says" erases schools | Theravāda named; commentarial and Āgama material labelled as such |

**Do not add:** a full Pali grammar course, an Abhidhamma survey, a
meditation manual, a history of Buddhism, or a comparative-religions track.
Each is a different product, and each would dilute the one thing this level
does: teach a person to read the canon.

---

## 9. Recommendation

Build **2.0 only**, as a pilot: Track A and two whole texts. It is the
cheapest way to learn whether the Level-1 reader actually wants to read a
3,000-word sutta, and it produces the renderer and schema that everything
else depends on.

If the pilot gate fails, the answer is not more content. It is that Level 1
is the product, and the advanced tier should be a reading list with a
protocol — not a second curriculum.
